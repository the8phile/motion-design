## Source story

### Scene 1 — The Mentor: Dr. Mbarga
Wednesday afternoon. The lecture hall smells of chalk dust and sun-warmed concrete, and the ceiling fans turn
slowly. Dr. Mbarga drops last year's business plans on the desk: coffee rings, crossed-out figures, staples bent
sideways. *"Good ideas,"* he says, *"and I could barely read them."* He writes the deadline on the board in
capitals: **FRIDAY, 10:00. TYPED. PRINTED. PRESENTED.** *"An investor gives you thirty seconds. If your pages
look careless, they'll assume your business is too. I will not accept handwriting."*

### Scene 2 — The Protagonist: Nadège
Nadège's laptop died in August, and the repair money went on fees. That night the power cuts out across the
neighbourhood, so she writes by the light of her phone torch. By morning she has eighteen pages in blue ink: a
plan to turn her mother's beignet stall into a real brand. The ideas are strong, but the pages are a mess.
Arrows run into the margins and there's a coffee stain right across the cash-flow table. She reads
Dr. Mbarga's words on the board again and gets it: the plan should look as serious as it is. Between classes she
photographs every page, opens **kopia.online**, chooses typing and printing for two copies, pays with MTN MoMo
and enters her lecture hall for delivery. Then she goes back to her seat.

### Scene 3 — The Kopia Brand Champion: Serge
At the partner print shop at the edge of campus, the order arrives with a soft chime. Serge pushes his reading
glasses up and puts Nadège's photos side by side on the screen. Where she drew arrows, he builds headings. The
cash-flow table that was crossed out three times becomes a clean grid, with every figure lined up. Her mother's
name sits in the title, spelled exactly right. He presses *Print*. The machine hums awake and sheets come out
one after another with a steady *shh-clack*, still warm, the black ink sharp. He knocks the stack square on the
counter, checks the first page against the last, and slides both copies into a clean sleeve. Later, in the
lecture hall, Nadège reads her PIN to the courier. It matches, and only then is her payment released. She lifts
the first page. It's still faintly warm.

### Scene 4 — The Proud Supporter: Maman Ekobena
Friday evening at the stall, oil crackles and the air smells of pepper and frying dough. Nadège holds out the
second copy. Her mother wipes her floury hands on her pagne before she'll touch it. *"I don't want to dirty it."*
She reads the title slowly: **Beignets Ekobena — Business Plan.** She runs a finger down the table of figures,
which are her own daily sales, now looking like a bank document. *"This is my stall?"* She calls the
neighbouring sellers over to see. Nadège tells her that Dr. Mbarga kept her after class to ask if he could use
her plan as next year's example. Her mother says nothing for a moment. Then she opens the cash box, counts out
the week's best takings and folds the notes into her daughter's hand. *"Your professor said investors give you
thirty seconds. I didn't need that long. Here's your first investment."*
